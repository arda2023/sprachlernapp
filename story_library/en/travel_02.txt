The last room

A date on a screen

By the time Maya reached the hotel, the rain had found its way through one shoulder of her coat. She left her suitcase beside the reception desk and tried to smile at the woman behind it.

"I have a reservation. Maya Bell. Two nights."

The woman typed, paused and asked how to spell Bell. Maya spelled it. Outside, a car crossed a puddle with a noise like somebody emptying a bucket.

"May I see your confirmation?"

Maya handed over her phone. She had already opened the email on the walk from the station. There was the hotel name, the room description and the price. Everything looked right until the woman pointed to the date.

November. Maya had booked November. It was October.

"Oh," she said.

The woman waited while Maya read it again, as if looking harder might move the reservation back a month.

"I'm afraid we're full tonight. There's a concert at the theatre."

Maya had come for the same concert. Her sister was playing the cello, and she had promised to be there before the doors opened. It had seemed sensible to arrive early, leave her bag and find somewhere for dinner. Instead, she was standing in a wet coat with a booking she could not use.

"Is there anywhere else nearby?"

"Let me make a call. I'm Elena, by the way. You can sit down."

Maya sat beneath a framed photograph of the town in summer. In the picture, people were eating ice cream beside a fountain. She took off her coat and discovered that her shirt was wet too.

Two possibilities

Elena called three places. At the first, she shook her head before putting down the receiver. The second was too far away without a car. On the third call, she wrote something on a small square of paper.

"There's one room at a guesthouse near the station. Shared bathroom. They can hold it for twenty minutes."

"I'll take it."

"Before you decide, we have another possibility. Our small room at the back is empty, but we haven't been selling it because work starts in the courtyard tomorrow. It will be noisy from seven."

Maya looked at the rain beyond the glass doors. The station was twenty minutes away in the opposite direction from the theatre.

"How noisy?"

Elena considered the question.

"I wouldn't promise you a pleasant morning. There are workers replacing the paving."

"Does the room have its own bathroom?"

"Yes. And a window, although the view is mostly scaffolding. I can show you before you choose."

The room was on the first floor. It contained a narrow bed, a wardrobe and a desk just wide enough for Maya's bag. The window faced a wall with a yellow cable hanging across it. Somewhere nearby, water dripped steadily into a metal container.

Maya checked the bathroom, tried the bedside light and looked at the clock. She had forty minutes before she needed to leave for the theatre.

"What would it cost?"

Elena gave her a lower price than the room she had booked for November. Maya could manage it. She asked Elena to release the guesthouse room so somebody else could have it.

"And my other reservation?"

"We can cancel that according to the conditions on your booking. I'll print the details when we go down."

An unexpected rehearsal

Twenty minutes later, Maya had a dry shirt, a room key and a message from her sister asking where she was.

She typed, "Nearly there," then deleted it.

"Wrong booking date. Found a room. Leaving now."

Her sister replied with a photograph of a cello case wedged between two chairs. Under it she had written, "At least your bed isn't backstage."

Maya laughed for the first time since arriving. At reception, Elena handed her the cancellation confirmation and a paper bag.

"A sandwich from the kitchen. You mentioned dinner."

"How much do I owe you?"

"Nothing. It's the last one, and the bread won't improve overnight."

Maya ate half while walking under the covered arcade. She reached the theatre just as an usher began checking tickets. Her sister spotted her from a doorway, lifted one hand and disappeared again. Maya had no time to explain the room. She found her seat with a small piece of lettuce still stuck to her sleeve.

The concert finished late. Afterwards, the sisters stood outside discussing a difficult passage that Maya had not noticed and her sister could not stop thinking about. They finally said goodnight when the theatre staff began switching off the entrance lights.

Seven in the morning

The first noise sounded as if someone had dropped a cupboard outside Maya's window. She opened one eye. Her phone said seven exactly.

Then came scraping, voices and a machine she could not identify. Elena had been accurate about the morning.

Maya dressed and went downstairs. In the breakfast room, Elena was arranging cups beside a coffee pot.

"Did you sleep?"

"Until seven. Very precisely."

"I'm sorry."

"You warned me. The concert was worth it."

There was a table free by the front window. Maya sat down with coffee and toast. Across the street, a baker was lifting the shutters. Her sister would not be awake for hours.

Maya opened her calendar and checked the train ticket home, slowly this time. Then she put the phone away. A small market was being assembled in the square, and she could see someone unloading boxes of pears.

Before going outside, she stopped at reception to ask about the following night.

"A courtyard room again?" Elena asked.

"Actually, if anyone leaves today, could I have a different view?"

Elena turned a page in her book.

"I think we can manage that."
