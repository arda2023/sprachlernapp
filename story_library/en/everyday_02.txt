The shelf in the hall

A place for everything

When Tessa came home, a cardboard box was standing in the middle of the living room. Her flatmate, Luis, sat beside it with an instruction sheet spread across his knees.

"I bought a shelf", he said.

"I can see that."

"For the hall. Shoes underneath, keys on top. No more piles by the door."

Tessa looked toward the hall. There were two pairs of her shoes, one pair of his and a shopping bag full of things neither of them had put away.

"Did you measure the space?"

"I measured the wall."

She put down her bag.

"That sounds like a carefully chosen answer."

Luis held up the instruction sheet.

"Help me build it, then you can complain about the measurements."

"I can do both."

They moved the coffee table and laid the wooden pieces on an old blanket. Tessa sorted the screws into small bowls while Luis identified the side panels. Neither could decide whether the drawing showed the finished shelf from the front or the back.

They turned the paper around. That did not help.

The wrong way round

After twenty minutes, the frame stood on the blanket. One smooth edge faced the wall, and one unfinished edge faced the room.

Tessa pointed at it.

"That should probably be the other way round."

Luis looked at the drawing again.

"Yes."

They loosened the screws, turned the panel and tightened them again. Tessa checked the remaining steps before they added the shelves.

"The low shelf is for shoes, right?"

"Yes."

"My boots won't fit."

"They can go beside it."

"Then we'll still have a pile beside the door."

Luis sat back on his heels.

"What do you want to put here?"

She considered the question. Until then, she had only been finding things wrong with his plan.

"My everyday shoes. The keys. Somewhere for letters that aren't bills."

"Why not bills?"

"Because I don't want the first thing I see when I come home to be a bill."

"Fair enough. My headphones need somewhere too."

They made a list on the back of the cardboard packaging. It was longer than the shelf was wide.

The door test

They carried the finished shelf into the hall and placed it against the wall. It fitted between the corner and the light switch with a little room to spare.

Luis stepped back.

"Measured correctly."

Tessa opened the front door. Halfway round, its handle touched the top of the shelf.

Luis closed his eyes.

"I measured the wall."

"You did say that."

They moved the shelf farther along. Now it stood in front of the cupboard where they kept the vacuum cleaner.

"We could stop vacuuming", Luis suggested.

"Your mother would still notice through a video call."

They put the shelf back in the living room and walked through the flat with the tape measure. Beside the sofa, there was enough space, but the shoes would look strange there. In the bedroom, it would fit perfectly, which did not solve their shared problem.

Tessa stopped at the end of the hall.

"What about here, instead of the little table?"

The table held a plant, a bowl of old keys and a lamp that had not worked since spring.

"Where would that go?" Luis asked.

"Let's find out what's actually on it first."

The things they kept

They carried the table into the living room and put everything on the floor.

The plant went by the window. The broken lamp went into a box for the next trip to the local repair event. Luis said he would check the event's date before promising to take it.

They examined the keys without throwing any away. One opened their old bicycle lock. Another belonged to Tessa's sister. Three remained unidentified.

"Those can stay in a labelled envelope until we know", Tessa said.

Under the bowl was a postcard Luis thought he had lost. It showed a mountain with clouds covering the summit.

"My dad sent this. He said the view was probably wonderful."

Tessa gave it to him, and he put it on his desk.

The little table, now empty, fitted beside the sofa. The new shelf fitted at the end of the hall. They could open the front door and the cupboard fully, and walk through without turning sideways.

Luis checked the assembly instructions once more and fitted the wall restraint as directed, using fixings suitable for their wall. Tessa waited until he had put the tools away before bringing the shoes over.

A week later

They each chose two pairs of shoes for the lower shelves. The others went into their own rooms. A small tray on top held the keys they actually used. Letters went in a narrow basket, and Luis put his headphones in the space beside it.

For three days, the shelf looked exactly as they had planned.

On the fourth, a supermarket receipt appeared beside the key tray. On the fifth, Tessa left a scarf across it. By Sunday, an empty bottle was standing where the headphones belonged.

Luis picked up the bottle.

"The shelf is becoming the old table."

Tessa took her scarf.

"Only if we let it."

They spent two minutes putting things away. Then Luis found the list they had written on the cardboard and laughed.

"We forgot one thing."

"What?"

"Somewhere for the tape measure."

Tessa opened the tool drawer in the kitchen.

"We already have that."

He put it back and closed the drawer. For once, the next person who needed it would not have to ask.
