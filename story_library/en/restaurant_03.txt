The family recipe

Three lines

The recipe occupied three lines in a notebook that smelled faintly of the cupboard where it had spent twenty years.

Onions. White beans. Tomatoes. Cook until ready.

Below that, in smaller writing, someone had added: Not too much vinegar.

"Very precise", said Rosa.

Her uncle Marco leaned over her shoulder.

"Your grandmother didn't need to write everything down."

"Unfortunately, I'm not her."

They stood in the kitchen of Marco's restaurant on a Monday afternoon, when it was closed to customers. Rosa had worked there for two months. She knew the lunch menu, the delivery schedule and which drawer stuck when the room got warm. She did not know how to make her grandmother's bean dish.

Marco wanted to serve it as a special on Friday. He had already written "A family favourite" at the top of a sheet of paper.

Rosa turned the notebook toward him.

"Before we advertise it, we need an actual recipe."

A small pot

They made a small batch first. Rosa weighed the ingredients while Marco searched the shelves for the earthenware dish his mother used to bring to the table.

"We're testing the food, not the plate", Rosa said.

"The plate matters."

"Then you can find it while I chop."

He found it behind a stack of serving bowls. There was a chip on the rim.

Rosa glanced at it.

"We can use a different dish for serving."

Marco ran a finger near the chip, then put it on a shelf away from the working surface.

They cooked the onions slowly and added the tomatoes and cooked beans. Rosa wrote down the amounts in a clean notebook. When Marco reached for the vinegar, she stopped him with her pen.

"How much?"

"A little."

"That isn't an amount."

He held a spoon over the pot. She measured what went into it before he poured.

They tasted the stew with separate spoons. Marco frowned.

"It isn't right."

"What isn't right?"

"I don't know."

Rosa tasted another small portion.

"I like it. But I can't compare it with a lunch I ate when I was six."

The phone call

Marco called his older sister, Bea. She answered with a shopping trolley rattling in the background.

"Did Mum put anything else in the beans?" he asked.

"Which beans?"

"The Sunday ones. With tomatoes."

"Sometimes carrots. Sometimes the last pepper in the fridge. Why?"

Marco looked at Rosa.

"We're trying to make her recipe."

"Which version?"

Rosa covered her mouth with the back of her hand. Marco turned away from her.

"The usual one."

"There wasn't a usual one. She used what she had. And you put cheese on yours before you even tasted it."

"I did not."

"You did. I sat next to you."

Bea paused while something beeped at the checkout.

"She usually added parsley at the end. Try that. I have to pay."

Marco ended the call. Rosa waited until he put the phone down.

"Should I add cheese before tasting?"

"You can add parsley."

They tried parsley in one small bowl and left another plain. Marco preferred the first. Rosa agreed that it tasted fresher, though she wanted a little less vinegar next time.

Something they could repeat

On Wednesday, Rosa made the dish again before lunch service. She used her notes, reduced the vinegar and kept the parsley separate until the end.

Marco tasted it.

"That's closer."

"To your memory or to what we want to serve?"

He took another spoonful.

"Both, I think."

Rosa wrote the final amount beside the vinegar. She also noted the portion size and how much bread they planned to serve with it.

Then she called Daniel, who would be serving tables on Friday, over to the counter.

"Tell me what you would need to know if someone ordered this."

He read the draft menu.

"Is it a starter or a main? Does the price include the bread? And what happens if somebody asks what's in it?"

Rosa showed him the ingredient sheet. They checked the ingredients of the bread and the products used in the stew, and kept that information with the restaurant's other dish records. If a customer asked about an allergy, Daniel would check the kitchen's information rather than guess from the name.

Marco crossed out "A family favourite".

"Why are you removing that?" Rosa asked.

"It doesn't tell anyone what they're ordering."

He wrote "White bean and tomato stew" instead. Below it, he added a short line about the recipe coming from his mother's kitchen.

Friday lunch

Bea arrived on Friday with a friend and asked for a table near the door. Marco came out of the kitchen before she had taken off her coat.

"You came to inspect us."

"I came for lunch. You told me to."

She ordered the beans. Her friend chose the fish.

Rosa stayed in the kitchen while Daniel carried the dishes out. She checked the next order twice, although it only asked for two soups and some extra bread.

When Daniel returned, she asked whether Bea had said anything.

"She asked for a spoon."

"About the food."

"Not yet."

After the lunch rush, Bea came to the kitchen doorway with her empty bowl.

"Very good", she said.

Marco folded his arms.

"Like Mum's?"

Bea looked from him to Rosa.

"Hers depended on what was left in the fridge. This one tastes like you planned it."

Rosa wasn't sure whether that was praise until Bea asked for the recipe.

"The old one or ours?"

"The one with amounts."

Rosa copied it onto a clean sheet. Marco put the old notebook back in its cupboard, then returned to add one sentence at the bottom of Bea's copy.

Serve with bread. Cheese optional.
