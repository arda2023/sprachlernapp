The wrong file

Before the meeting

At twenty past eight, Tom had one hand on his coat and the other on his mouse. He worked in the office of a community centre, and the coordinator was waiting for the programme of Saturday's activities.

The meeting started at nine. His bus left in six minutes.

He attached the file, wrote a short message and pressed send.

Then he picked up his bag, put down his bag, and went back for his lunch.

On the bus, he remembered something less useful than lunch. There were two copies of the programme on his computer. One was in the shared folder. The other was on his desktop, where he had saved it the previous afternoon before the pottery teacher called.

The pottery class had moved from eleven to twelve. The room had changed too.

Tom opened his sent message on his phone. The attachment was called Saturday Programme.

That did not help. Both copies had the same name.

He opened the attachment itself. Beside pottery, it said eleven, room two.

The first call

Tom looked at the clock. It was eight thirty four. He could wait until he reached the office and explain in person. Perhaps nobody had opened the message yet.

Then he imagined twelve copies of the wrong programme on the meeting table.

He called the coordinator, Eva.

"I've sent you yesterday's version," he said. "The pottery time and room are wrong. Please don't print that attachment."

"I already printed one copy. Is it only the pottery class?"

"That's the change I remember. I need to compare the files before I say it's the only one. I'll be there in about ten minutes."

"All right. I'll mark this copy as old. Check the rest when you arrive."

Tom put his phone away. He had expected a longer conversation, perhaps a disappointed silence. Instead, Eva had asked a practical question, and he had not yet given her a complete answer.

At the next stop, a passenger squeezed past him with a folded pushchair. Tom moved his bag and nearly left his lunch on the seat.

Two versions

In the office, he took off his coat and opened both files side by side.

The pottery class was not the only difference. The walking group now met at the side entrance because the main hall would be used for a sale. He had forgotten that change altogether.

He checked the revised programme against the messages from the activity leaders. Then he asked his colleague Nia to read the times and room numbers aloud while he followed the list.

"You're very quiet this morning," she said.

"I'm trying not to make a second mistake while fixing the first."

Nia pulled her chair closer.

They found no further differences. Tom exported a fresh copy with the revision date in its name, opened that exported file and checked it once more.

At eight fifty two, he sent it to Eva. His message named the two corrections and asked her to use this attachment instead of the earlier one.

Then he walked to the meeting room with a printed copy.

Eva was arranging chairs.

"This is the replacement?"

"Yes. Pottery at twelve in room four. The walking group meets at the side entrance. Everything else matches the confirmed list."

She crossed out the front page of the old copy and put it in the paper recycling box.

One more place

After the meeting, Tom felt ready for coffee. Nia stopped him at the kitchen door.

"Did the desk get the first version too?"

He stood still.

He had sent it to reception late the previous day, when the afternoon changes were still arriving. They kept a copy for people who asked about weekend activities.

"I'll check."

At reception, a yellow sheet lay beside the phone. Tom recognised his old programme. He explained the corrections to Jo, who worked there that morning, and replaced the sheet.

"Has anyone asked about pottery?" he said.

Jo looked at the notes beside her keyboard.

"One caller. I told her eleven. She asked me to reserve a place, so I have her number in the booking system. I'll call and explain the change."

Tom waited while she made the call. The new time suited the caller. Jo updated the booking note and turned back to him.

"Anything else changing?"

"Nothing confirmed. If something does, I'll send you a replacement and say exactly what's different."

A smaller folder

That afternoon, Tom and Nia looked at the shared folder. It contained six files with Saturday in the name. Two included the word final.

Neither was the version they had used that morning.

They agreed to keep the current programme in one clearly labelled folder and move older versions into an archive. They would use dates in exported filenames and check the attachment before sending it. Eva agreed to the arrangement.

Tom moved his desktop copy into the archive as well. For a moment, the empty space on the screen looked oddly noticeable.

At five, he sent Nia the confirmed programme for the following staff shift. He opened the attachment from the draft message before pressing send.

"Twelve o'clock," he said quietly. "Room four. Side entrance."

Nia looked up.

"Talking to the computer?"

"Checking what it plans to tell people."

He shut it down, picked up his bag and reached for his coat.

Then he remembered his lunch. It was still in the office fridge, exactly where he had put it that morning.
