Someone else's letter

The blue envelope

The blue envelope lay between a supermarket leaflet and a bill in Maya's letterbox. Her address was on the front, but the name was not hers.

Helen Ward.

Maya had lived in the flat for three weeks. She still received letters for the previous tenant, whose name was David. This was someone new.

She carried everything upstairs and put the envelope on the kitchen table without opening it. A corner had been decorated with a small drawing of a lemon. The handwriting leaned sharply to the right.

Her own grandmother wrote like that. Maya almost called her, then remembered that Thursday was her swimming day. She wrote herself a reminder instead.

At six, she took the blue envelope downstairs again.

The names beside the doorbells were printed neatly behind plastic covers. No Ward. On the lowest button, someone had written "Please press firmly" in tiny letters.

Maya pressed the button for the building manager.

A similar number

Mr Bell opened his door carrying a roll of tape. There was a cardboard box at his feet and another balanced on the washing machine behind him.

"Sorry to bother you. Do you know a Helen Ward? This came to my flat."

He looked at the name, then the address.

"Not in this building. What number does it say?"

"Fourteen."

He moved the envelope closer to the hall light.

"Are you sure that isn't forty one?"

Maya looked again. The first number had a long sloping stroke. The second had a faint mark across it.

"It could be."

"Forty one is across the road, near the corner. You could check the name there."

He did not know whether Helen lived there, and said so. Maya thanked him and tucked the envelope inside her jacket to keep it out of the drizzle.

At number forty one, the doorbells had no plastic covers. Three names were written directly on little brass plates. The middle one said Ward.

She rang.

The person at the door

A woman opened the door with a tea towel over one shoulder.

"Yes?"

"Hello. I'm sorry, I think your letter was delivered to my building. Are you Helen Ward?"

"I am."

Maya held out the envelope.

"I'm at fourteen. The numbers looked a bit alike."

Helen looked at the handwriting and laughed.

"My brother. He writes everything as if the pen is trying to escape. Thank you for bringing it over."

"I wasn't sure where it belonged."

"Well, it found me."

Helen opened it while Maya was turning to leave.

"Oh, wait."

She unfolded a card with the same lemon drawing on it.

"He's inviting me to dinner on Sunday. He's written the date but not the time."

"Maybe it's on the other side."

Helen turned the card over.

"No. Just a larger lemon."

Maya laughed. Helen shook her head and put the card back in the envelope.

"Are you new at fourteen?"

"Three weeks."

"Then you won't know which day they collect the recycling."

"I thought it was yesterday. Nobody else put anything out, so I brought mine back in."

"That's what happens when you follow the old notice in your hallway."

The notice

Helen fetched the current collection calendar from just inside her door. She showed Maya the dates for their street and let her take a photograph.

"The schedule changed last month. Your building probably still has the old sheet."

"It does. I read it twice."

"Reading the wrong information carefully is still reading the wrong information. I do it with bus timetables."

Maya put her phone away.

"I'll tell Mr Bell. He's downstairs, packing something."

"Probably the books for the school sale. He asked everyone for one box and got eleven."

They stood under the small porch while a cyclist passed through a puddle. Maya stepped farther from the pavement.

"Would you like to come in for tea?" Helen asked.

"Thank you, but I've left my shopping upstairs. There are things I should put in the fridge."

"Another time, then."

"I'd like that."

Helen lifted the envelope.

"And next time you won't need an excuse to knock."

Back at fourteen

Mr Bell was still surrounded by boxes when Maya returned. She showed him the calendar on her phone.

"Helen at forty one has the current dates. The notice here is from before the change."

He put down the tape.

"That explains Tuesday. I thought everyone had gone away."

He checked the date on the sheet in the hall, then removed it. Maya offered to send him the photograph. He gave her the email address he used for building matters and said he would print a replacement.

She helped him move one box away from his doorway before going upstairs. It was heavier than it looked.

"Books?"

"Cookbooks. Apparently nobody wants to make anything complicated anymore."

In her flat, Maya put the milk and cheese away. The blue envelope had left a dry rectangle on the table where the rest of the surface was still damp from her shopping bag.

She wiped it, made some toast and called her grandmother.

"How was swimming?"

"Cancelled. The pool had a problem. I nearly called you, but I thought you'd be busy."

Maya sat down.

"I'm not busy now."

They talked until the toast was cold. Before hanging up, Maya asked whether her grandmother would like to visit on Sunday afternoon.

"What time?"

Maya looked at the empty place on the table where the letter had been.

"Three. I'll write it down properly."
