The missing order

Before the film

At six thirty, Nina put two cinema tickets on the restaurant table and held them flat with the salt cellar.

"The film starts at eight. We have plenty of time."

Her friend Joel took off his scarf.

"You said that last time."

"Last time you went to the wrong cinema."

"It had the same name."

"It was in another town."

He hung the scarf over his chair and picked up the menu. They had not seen each other in six weeks. Joel had been working evenings, and Nina had spent most weekends helping her sister move. She had bought the tickets before either of them could suggest another date.

The restaurant was a short walk from the cinema. From their table near the counter, they could see people carrying folded umbrellas through the door. Water collected on the mat.

A server named Eva brought two glasses and a jug of water.

"Are you ready to order?"

Joel chose the mushroom pasta. Nina ordered the bean stew with rice.

"And a side salad to share, please", Joel added.

Eva repeated the order, including the salad, and took their menus.

One plate

The salad came first. Joel divided it between two small plates while Nina told him about a wardrobe that would not fit up her sister's stairs.

"What did you do?"

"Took it apart."

"Sensible."

"After we'd carried it up three floors."

He was laughing when Eva arrived with the pasta. She placed it in front of him, then turned toward the kitchen as someone called her name.

Nina moved her glass aside to make room for her own plate.

Nothing else arrived.

Joel waited with his fork in his hand.

"Eat before it gets cold", she said.

"Yours will be here in a second."

They finished the story about the wardrobe. Joel took two bites of pasta. A server brought food to the table behind them, then returned to the counter with an empty tray.

Nina checked the time. Seven ten.

"I'll ask", she said.

Joel looked toward the kitchen door.

"They're busy."

"I know. I only want to find out."

She caught Eva's eye and raised her hand slightly. Eva came over.

"Excuse me. Could you check on the bean stew? We ordered it with the pasta."

Eva looked at Nina's empty place.

"Of course. I'm sorry. I'll find out now."

The kitchen ticket

When Eva returned, she held the small pad on which she had written their order.

"Your stew is here on my notes, but I missed it when I entered the order. The kitchen hasn't started it. I'm sorry."

Nina looked at the cinema tickets beneath the salt cellar.

"How long would it take?"

"About fifteen minutes, according to the cook. I don't want to promise less."

It was seven fifteen. They needed to leave by seven forty to walk to the cinema and find their seats without rushing.

Joel lowered his fork.

"Could we have it to take away?"

"I don't want to carry stew into a cinema", Nina said.

"Right. No."

Eva waited while Nina thought.

"Is there anything that would be ready sooner?"

"I can check. Would you like me to ask about the soup?"

"Yes, please. Does it come with bread?"

"It does."

Eva went back to the kitchen and returned with an answer rather than another guess.

"The vegetable soup is ready. We can bring a bowl with bread in about five minutes. You can also cancel your main course if you'd rather leave."

Nina was hungry. She had eaten a banana at her desk and called it lunch.

"I'll have the soup. Please cancel the stew."

Eva repeated the change and went to enter it.

Still at the table

Joel pushed the salad bowl toward Nina. There were a few leaves and one tomato left.

"Do you want the rest?"

"Yes. And please eat your pasta. Watching it go cold isn't helping either of us."

He took another forkful.

"I wasn't sure whether you wanted me to say something."

"I can ask about my dinner."

"I know you can."

She picked up the last tomato.

"I was annoyed. Now I know what's happening. That's better."

Joel nodded. Then he asked whether her sister had managed to rebuild the wardrobe.

"Mostly. There's one screw left."

"There's always one screw left."

"She put it in a drawer. Apparently that makes it a spare."

The soup arrived while he was still smiling. Eva set down the bowl and a basket of bread.

"The stew is cancelled. I've also taken the soup off your bill because of the wait. I'm sorry your meals didn't arrive together."

"Thank you for sorting it out", Nina said.

The soup was hot. She broke the bread into pieces and let the first spoonful cool. Joel finished his pasta while she ate. They still had time to discuss which of the two films on the poster outside looked worse.

Checking the bill

At seven thirty, Nina asked for the bill. It listed the pasta and the salad. Neither the stew nor the soup appeared.

Joel checked it too, then reached for his wallet.

"We said we'd split it, but that doesn't make much sense now. I'll pay for the pasta."

"And half the salad", Nina said.

"A very important half."

They paid at the counter. Eva was helping another table, so Nina thanked the person taking the payment and collected the tickets.

Outside, Joel stopped under the awning and searched his coat pockets.

Nina held up both tickets.

"I have them."

"My scarf."

She watched him hurry back inside. Through the window, she saw Eva lift the scarf from his chair and hand it to him.

When he came out, Nina checked the time.

"Seven thirty eight. We're still fine."

Joel wrapped the scarf around his neck.

"As long as it's the right cinema."

She turned the tickets toward him so he could read the address.
