A difficult handover

Ten minutes together

When Priya arrived at the bicycle shop, Dan was talking on the phone, a customer was waiting at the counter and a small dog was licking the leg of a display stand.

Their shifts overlapped by ten minutes. Usually, that was enough.

Dan covered the phone with his hand.

"Blue bike's ready. Red one needs a call. Delivery at three. Everything's in the book."

Priya put her bag under the counter.

"Which blue bike?"

There were two near the collection area. One had a basket. The other had a child seat.

Dan pointed, but the customer stepped between them at the same moment. Priya saw a finger and the back of a coat.

Then Dan returned to his call.

The waiting customer wanted a new bell. Priya showed him three, let him try them and explained how they fitted. His dog stopped licking the stand and looked offended by each sound.

By the time the customer left, seven of their ten minutes had gone.

The open book

Dan picked up his jacket.

"Sorry. I need to catch the bus. Can you call if anything's unclear?"

Priya looked at the open notebook. Under red bike, someone had written, "Ask about wheel."

"One minute before you go. I can't tell what this means."

Dan put the jacket down again.

"The customer needs to approve a replacement wheel."

"Have we given her a price?"

"I think so. Ask Mark."

Mark, the mechanic, was behind the workshop door. Priya could hear a pump and a radio.

She turned back a page. There were several crossed out entries and a phone number with no name beside it. She did not know whether it belonged to the owner of the red bicycle.

"Let's use the job numbers," she said. "Otherwise I might call the wrong person."

Dan opened the repair system on the shop computer.

They found the red bicycle under job forty two. The customer's number was there, along with Mark's estimate. No approval had been recorded.

"So I call her with the estimate, and Mark waits for her answer?"

"Yes. That's it."

Priya wrote the next action beside the job number, then added her own name.

Which one is ready?

The blue bicycle with the basket was job thirty eight. Its repair was finished and the safety check was recorded. The customer had been told she could collect it after two.

The blue bicycle with the child seat was still waiting for a part.

Dan looked at the two bikes.

"I should have said basket."

"The number is better. We might have three baskets tomorrow."

He smiled, but Priya could see him glance at the clock.

"I'll finish the notes," she said. "Tell me about the delivery."

It was a box of inner tubes, expected around three, not a bicycle delivery. The order reference was in the system. Priya needed to check the quantity against the delivery note before signing and put the box on the stock shelf.

She repeated the three tasks aloud. Dan corrected one detail: the tubes belonged on the lower shelf, not the upper one, which held returned items.

Then he left at his scheduled time. Priya still had a page of untidy notes, but she knew what each line meant.

A question for the workshop

Before calling the owner of the red bicycle, Priya went to find Mark.

"The estimate says the wheel and fitting are included. Is there anything else I need to explain?"

Mark stopped pumping a tyre.

"The wheel has to be ordered. If she agrees today, I'll confirm a collection date once the supplier confirms delivery. Don't promise tomorrow."

Priya added that to her notes.

The customer answered on the fourth ring. Priya gave the price and explained the timing. The customer asked whether she could think about it until the next morning.

"Of course. I'll record that we're waiting for your decision. No replacement wheel will be ordered yet."

After the call, Priya updated the repair record and told Mark. She crossed the completed call off her own list, leaving the customer's decision clearly marked as pending.

At quarter past two, the owner of the blue bicycle arrived. Priya checked the job number and brought out the bicycle with the basket.

The basket contained one glove.

"I've been looking for that all week," the customer said.

Priya decided not to mention that the bicycle had only been in the shop since yesterday.

The next afternoon

The delivery arrived twenty minutes late. Priya checked it, put it on the correct shelf and recorded that it had arrived. At closing time, she could see which tasks were finished without trying to remember every conversation.

When Dan came in the next day, he brought two coffees.

"One is an apology for the blue bike instructions."

"Which one?"

He looked down at the identical cups, then laughed.

"The one without sugar. Wait, let me check."

Over coffee, they agreed on a short handover routine: job number, current situation, next action and who would do it. Anything unfinished would stay visible in the repair system.

They also asked the manager for five protected minutes together before each shift ended. One person would cover the counter while the other two handed over.

That afternoon, Dan's first note began with a job number. Priya read it and asked one question. He answered it, put on his jacket and caught his bus.
