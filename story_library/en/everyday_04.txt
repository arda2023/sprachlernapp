The new neighbour

The chair upstairs

At six forty on Monday morning, a chair scraped across the ceiling above Iris's bed.

She opened one eye. Another scrape followed, then a soft thump. After that, silence.

The flat upstairs had been empty for two months. Someone had moved in on Saturday, but Iris had only seen the backs of two people carrying a mattress. She knew nothing else about her new neighbour except that he owned a chair and got up earlier than she did.

She turned the pillow over and tried to sleep.

On Tuesday, the same sound came at six forty five. On Wednesday, it came while she was already awake, waiting for it.

That annoyed her more than the noise itself.

She composed a message in her head while brushing her teeth. It began with "Some of us are trying to sleep" and became less polite from there.

By the time she left for work, she had decided not to put that message through anyone's door.

On the stairs

That evening, Iris met a man on the landing carrying a small plant and a bag of oranges. One orange rolled from the bag and stopped against her shoe.

She picked it up.

"I think this is yours."

"Thank you. The bag has decided it's finished."

He shifted the plant to his other arm. Iris held the torn bag while he found his keys.

"I'm Noah. I've just moved in upstairs."

"Iris. Directly below you."

He smiled.

"Then I'm sorry about Saturday. The mattress was more difficult than expected."

"I was out for most of it."

She almost left the conversation there. He seemed tired, and the plant was slowly sliding down his sleeve. But she did not want another morning of lying in bed, angry with someone who had no idea why.

"There is one thing, actually. I hear something dragging across the floor early in the morning. Around quarter to seven. It wakes me up."

Noah frowned, looking up toward his flat as if the answer might be visible through the ceiling.

"My kitchen chair, probably. I eat before leaving for work."

"It sounds quite loud downstairs."

"I didn't realise. Thank you for telling me."

A small experiment

Noah put the plant and oranges inside, then came back to the landing.

"Could we check? I can move the chair while you're in your flat. Then we'll know whether that's it."

Iris agreed. They exchanged numbers for the test, and she went downstairs. In her bedroom, she sent a message saying she was ready.

The familiar scrape crossed the ceiling.

"That's it", she wrote.

A moment later, a second sound came, softer and shorter.

"I lifted it that time", Noah replied.

She could still hear a small bump, but nothing like the first noise.

They met on the landing again.

"The kitchen must be right over your room", he said. "I'll get pads for the chair legs. Until then, I'll lift it."

"Thank you. I don't expect you to stop having breakfast."

"Good. I'm not pleasant without it."

She smiled. The speech she had prepared that morning would have sounded ridiculous now.

What the walls carried

On Friday, Noah sent a short message asking whether the chair was better.

Iris had forgotten to listen for it.

"Yes. Much better. Thank you."

She was about to put the phone away when another message arrived.

"Can I ask something too? Is there a television against the wall in your living room?"

There was. Iris looked at it.

"Yes. Can you hear it?"

"Mostly the low sounds. Last night I thought someone was moving furniture until I heard the music."

She had watched a film with a long scene involving a helicopter. The television had been playing through a small speaker on the floor.

"That's probably mine. I'll move the speaker and lower the bass. Let me know if it's still a problem."

After changing the settings, she played a short part of the same scene at her usual volume. Noah listened upstairs and messaged that it was much less noticeable.

Iris put the speaker on a suitable stand away from their shared wall. She also lowered the volume for the rest of the evening. She had been so busy noticing his chair that she had never wondered what he could hear from her flat.

The spare oranges

On Sunday afternoon, there was a knock at Iris's door. Noah held a small bowl of oranges.

"A replacement for the one you rescued. Plus some extras. I bought too many."

"You don't owe me an orange."

"Then consider it help with a storage problem."

She took the bowl and invited him in for coffee. This time, he was not carrying a plant, and neither of them needed to hurry out to work.

Noah asked where she bought bread. Iris told him about the bakery near the bus stop, including the fact that it closed early on Sundays. He told her that the small hardware shop had the chair pads behind the counter, not on the shelves where he had spent ten minutes looking.

When he left, Iris washed the bowl and dried it. She put two slices of the cake she had made inside, then carried it upstairs.

Noah opened the door.

"The bowl is becoming a delivery service", he said.

"Only until one of us stops returning it."

From behind him came the sound of a chair being lifted and set down carefully. His visiting sister appeared in the kitchen doorway.

"Like that?" she asked.

Noah looked at Iris.

"She's learning the local customs."
