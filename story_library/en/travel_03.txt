A stop by the sea

Twenty minutes

"We could get off here," said Leo.

His mother looked up from the route on her phone. Through the bus window, she could see a strip of water between two buildings. A blue sign announced the harbour. Beyond it, gulls stood on a roof with their heads turned into the wind.

"Our tickets are for the next town."

"I know. But we haven't actually been near the sea yet."

Nora glanced at the time. They were supposed to reach the apartment at four. The owner had asked them to send a message when they were close. She had already planned a supermarket stop and worked out which bus to take the following morning.

Leo had spent most of the journey watching the water appear and disappear behind houses. At fifteen, he rarely asked for anything twice. Usually he put on his headphones and let the question go.

"How long until the next bus?" Nora asked.

They checked the timetable beside the rear door. Another service would come in an hour and a half.

"We could walk down for twenty minutes," she said.

Leo pressed the stop button before she changed her mind.

The closed gate

They put their backpacks on and followed a narrow street downhill. Nora messaged the apartment owner to say they would arrive later. The reply came while she was still typing an explanation: "No problem. Keys are in the box. I'll send the code."

She put the phone away.

At the bottom of the street, a fence separated them from the harbour. A gate stood open, but a sign said the working quay was closed to visitors. Behind it, two people were moving crates from a boat.

"Can we go around?" Leo asked.

A woman carrying a coil of rope pointed back up the street.

"Public beach is on the other side. Take the steps beside the bakery, then turn left."

Nora thanked her. Leo looked at the water beyond the gate before following.

"It's always farther than it looks," he said.

"Especially downhill. You forget you have to come back up."

At the bakery, they bought two rolls and asked whether there was a place to leave their bags. The man behind the counter shook his head apologetically. Nora bought a bottle of water as well, and they carried everything down the steps.

A bench without a view

The beach was small and mostly pebbles. A concrete path ran along the back, with three benches facing the water. The first was wet, the second occupied, and the third faced a large waste container that someone had parked in front of it.

Leo dropped his backpack beside the third bench.

"Perfect sea view."

Nora laughed and sat down. Her shoulders were sore. She had packed a second pair of shoes because the weather might change. At the moment, she disliked both pairs.

Leo took his roll to the low wall farther along the path. Nora watched him balance it on its paper bag while he tried to photograph a bird. Each time he raised the phone, the bird walked behind a post.

She checked the time. Their twenty minutes had nearly gone.

"We should think about heading back," she called.

"We've only just got here."

"We still need to find the right bus stop."

He put the phone down.

"You said we could stop."

"And we did."

The words sounded less reasonable once she had said them. Leo looked away. Nora took a bite of her roll, though she wasn't hungry anymore.

She had organized the whole trip. Trains, the apartment, tickets for a museum he had mentioned months earlier. She wanted him to enjoy it. Yet most of what she had said that day involved times, bags or where they needed to be next.

"Come here a minute," she said.

He stayed by the wall.

"I'm not asking you to leave. Let's look at the timetable together."

A different plan

They spread the paper bus timetable between them. Nora held one corner down with the water bottle. Leo followed the line of stops with his finger.

"This one goes from the square. We passed it."

"And after that?"

"Another hour. Then there's one more."

Nora checked the apartment message again. There was no one waiting at a desk, no appointment they would miss. The supermarket stayed open into the evening. They could take the later bus and still arrive with time to buy dinner.

"Would another hour be enough?"

Leo nodded, then looked at her backpack.

"You don't have to walk around. I can stay where you can see me."

"I'd quite like to see something other than that container."

They moved to the wall. Nora folded her coat to sit on, and Leo offered her half of his roll. It was the same as hers, but she accepted it anyway.

For a while, he took photographs. Then he showed her three: a gull, the empty harbour entrance and her face just as she had discovered the bench's view.

"Delete that last one."

"It's the best one."

"Then at least send it to me."

The journey continues

They left with enough time to climb the steps slowly. At the bakery, Nora bought two pastries for later. The man recognized them and asked whether they had found the beach.

"Eventually," Leo said.

The bus stop in the square was easy to find. While they waited, Nora handed Leo the address of the apartment.

"You're in charge of telling me where we get off."

He read it, found the stop on the timetable and put an alarm on his phone.

On the bus, Nora took the window seat. Leo sat beside her with his headphones around his neck. As they pulled away, the sea appeared once more between the buildings.

This time, she was looking when it came into view.
