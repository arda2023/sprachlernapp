The last guest

The sign on the door

At nine twenty, Amir stopped wiping the counter because someone was reading the sign outside.

The woman wore a dark coat and carried a violin case. She looked at the opening hours, checked her phone and opened the door just far enough to speak.

"Are you still serving food?"

"We take kitchen orders until half past nine", Amir said. "The restaurant closes at ten."

"Then I'm not too late?"

"Not if you order soon. Let me check what's left."

He showed her a small table near the counter. She set the violin case beneath it, carefully away from the aisle.

The dining room was nearly empty. At the far end, a couple were finishing coffee. Rain tapped against the front window. In the kitchen, someone moved a tray with a long metallic scrape.

Amir took a menu to the table.

"The fish and the vegetable tart have sold out. I'll ask about the other dishes."

The woman looked down the page.

"I'm happy with something simple."

"I'll still find out before you choose."

What was left

Marta, the cook, was counting the portions in a shallow container.

"Two chicken dishes, one lentil stew. Soup as well", she said. "The potatoes are finished. We can do rice."

Amir repeated the list before going back to the table.

"The chicken comes with rice tonight. There's also lentil stew or vegetable soup with bread."

"The stew, please. And water."

"Still or sparkling?"

"Still. Can I ask how long the food will take?"

"I'll check with Marta."

The woman smiled.

"You do a lot of checking."

"It saves me apologising later."

Marta said the stew would be ready in about ten minutes. Amir entered the order and returned with the water.

"About ten minutes. Is that all right?"

"Yes, thank you. I'm Celia, by the way. I've just come from the hall around the corner."

She touched the handle of the violin case with her shoe.

"Long rehearsal."

Amir glanced at the case.

"The concert's tomorrow, isn't it?"

"Tomorrow and Saturday. Tonight we mostly practised stopping and starting."

The phone on the table

While Celia waited, Amir cleared the coffee cups from the other table. The couple paid and left, taking the sound of their conversation out into the rain.

Celia's phone rang. She answered, listened and reached for the little paper programme sticking out of her coat pocket.

"No, the side entrance. I'll come back. Give me a few minutes."

She ended the call and looked toward Amir.

"I'm sorry. Can I change that to take away?"

He checked the order screen.

"It hasn't left the kitchen yet. I'll ask them to pack it."

"The porter has found a bag of music we left behind. He's locking the hall soon."

Amir went to the kitchen. Marta had a bowl ready beside the pot, but she set it aside and reached for a food container.

"Bread too?"

"Yes, please. Separately, if possible."

He returned to Celia.

"We can pack it. The stew will be hot, so keep the container upright."

She looked at her violin case, then at the programme and phone she was trying to put away.

"This may require more hands."

A bag with handles

Amir brought a paper carrier bag. Celia placed the violin case on the chair beside her while she freed a hand from the sleeve of her coat.

"I always think I can carry one more thing", she said.

"Then the door needs opening."

"Exactly."

Marta brought the packed stew to the counter with the bread in a separate bag. Amir checked the lid, placed the container flat in the carrier and added a spoon and napkin.

"Would you like the water bottle as well?"

"Yes, please."

He placed it beside the container so it would stay upright. Celia paid and asked for the receipt.

"Could you put it in the bag?"

"Of course."

At the door, she paused.

"Would it be possible to come back and eat here after I collect the music?"

Amir looked at the clock. Nine thirty eight.

"We close at ten. If you're back before then, you can use the table until ten. We won't be able to take another kitchen order."

"That's fair. If I'm delayed, I'll take it home."

He held the door while she stepped into the rain.

Ten minutes later

Celia returned with the violin case, the carrier bag and a large blue folder tucked under her arm. She stopped inside the entrance to look at the clock.

"Twelve minutes. Is that enough?"

"The table is still yours until ten."

She sat down, put the folder on the empty chair and took the food container out of the bag.

Amir brought a proper spoon.

"You can save the other one."

"Thank you. I was expecting to eat this standing in my kitchen."

He continued wiping the counter. Marta finished cleaning the kitchen and brought out the list for the next morning's delivery. Neither stood over Celia while she ate.

At five to ten, Celia closed the empty container and gathered her things.

"That was exactly what I needed."

"Glad you made it back."

She took one programme from her coat and placed it on the counter.

"The concert information, if you're interested. No obligation to come and listen to us start and stop. We'll try to get through the whole thing tomorrow."

After she left, Amir checked her table. Nothing was underneath it. No case, no folder, no forgotten phone.

Marta read the programme while he turned the door sign around.

"Saturday starts at seven", she said.

"We're working."

She pointed to a smaller line near the bottom.

"There's an afternoon performance too."

Amir dried his hands and leaned closer to read the time.
