Saturday without power

The quiet kitchen

The washing machine stopped halfway through a turn. A moment later, the radio fell silent.

Elena stood in the kitchen with a spoon in her hand. She pressed the radio button, then looked at the blank display on the oven.

"Oh, no."

Saturday was meant to be her useful day. She had a load of washing underway, bread waiting for the toaster and a long list beside her laptop. By lunchtime, she intended to have done everything she had avoided during the week.

Now even the clock on the oven had given up.

She switched off the toaster and the other appliances she had been using. Through the window, she could see her neighbour Paul standing in his kitchen, looking at his own ceiling light.

He noticed her and spread his hands.

Elena pointed at the dark radio. He nodded.

At least it wasn't only her flat.

At the front door

They met downstairs a few minutes later. Paul was carrying his phone and wearing slippers that were not meant for the wet pavement.

"Have you heard anything?" he asked.

"Not yet. I'm checking the electricity company's outage page."

The page loaded slowly over her mobile connection. It showed a fault affecting several nearby streets. The estimated repair time was early afternoon, with a note that it could change.

She showed Paul.

"So we're having a quiet morning", he said.

"I had plans."

"So did I. Mine involved a drill, which might have disturbed yours."

Elena looked up from the phone.

"Then perhaps this isn't entirely bad."

They went back inside. Paul offered to tell Mrs Reed on the ground floor, who rarely used her mobile phone. Elena said she would write the update on paper for the noticeboard, including the time she had checked it.

"Put the company name on it too", Paul said. "Otherwise it looks as if you've personally promised to fix the street by lunch."

A different breakfast

Upstairs, Elena left the fridge and freezer closed. The bread on the counter would have to do without toasting. She spread jam on a slice and ate it while staring at the list beside her laptop.

Wash clothes. Send forms. Clean kitchen. Buy birthday card.

She could still clean the kitchen. She did not want to.

There was a knock at the door.

Paul held up a small packet.

"Biscuits. Mrs Reed says we should have these because she bought the wrong kind."

"What kind did she want?"

"Apparently any kind except these."

Elena took one. It was plain and a little broken at the edge.

"They're fine."

"That's what I said. She gave me the whole packet."

He glanced at her table.

"You're still working through the list?"

"Mostly looking at it."

"I have a proposal. The postbox still works. I need to send a letter, and you look as if you could use a walk."

She looked at the last item.

"I need a birthday card."

"There we are. Two people with somewhere to go."

The long way round

Paul changed his slippers for shoes. Elena put her laptop away and took a small bag. They checked that their doors were locked and walked toward the shops beyond the affected streets.

At the first corner, Paul turned left.

"The shops are the other way", Elena said.

"The park is this way. It's only ten minutes longer."

She nearly said she didn't have time. Then she remembered the washing machine sitting silently at home.

"All right."

The park path was wet, but the rain had stopped. A child in a red coat was trying to persuade a small dog to cross a shallow puddle. The dog went around it instead.

"Sensible", Paul said.

At the card shop, Elena spent longer choosing than she expected. Most of the cards made jokes about getting older. Her brother was turning thirty and had already heard enough of those.

She chose a card with a badly drawn bicycle on it. He had recently bought a bicycle and talked about it as if it were a new family member.

Paul bought a pen because the letter he wanted to post still needed signing.

They sat on a bench outside the shop. Elena wrote her card while Paul signed the letter against the flat cover of his notebook.

"What did people use before phones?" she asked as he looked for an address.

He held up the notebook.

"This remarkably advanced device."

The afternoon update

They bought sandwiches from a shop with power and ate them in the park. Before going home, Elena checked the outage page again. The repair estimate had moved back by an hour.

She sighed, then sent a short message to the person expecting her forms. She would send them later that day if the power returned, and update them if it did not.

Paul watched her put the phone away.

"That's one thing dealt with."

"It's not sent."

"No. But they know why."

Back at the building, Elena replaced the notice with the new information. Mrs Reed opened her door and asked whether they had found coffee.

"Sandwiches", Paul said.

"Probably more useful."

At half past two, Elena heard the fridge start again. She checked the company's update, then removed the notice downstairs once the supply was restored. Back in her flat, she restarted the washing machine according to its instructions and sent the forms.

The birthday card was already written and posted. That usually took her three separate attempts.

A few minutes later, her phone lit up with a message from Paul.

"Power's back. Is now a terrible time for the drill?"

She looked at the clean patch of kitchen counter she had finally wiped.

"Give me ten minutes to find my headphones."
