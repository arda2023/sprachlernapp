The missing cat

An empty chair

Milo was not on the kitchen chair. He was not behind the curtain, under the bed or in the laundry basket, where he sometimes slept on clean towels.

Sara checked the basket twice.

"Milo?"

Her cat usually appeared as soon as she opened the cupboard containing his food. This time, the sound brought nobody.

She had been carrying bags in from the car that morning. The front door had stood open for a few minutes while her cousin Ben helped with a heavy box. Milo might have slipped out then.

Or he might still be asleep somewhere she had not looked.

Sara searched the flat carefully, including the cupboard she had opened while putting things away. Ben checked behind the sofa, then straightened up with a dusty tennis ball.

"Not a cat."

"I can see that."

He put the ball on the table.

"Let's work out where we haven't looked."

A clear description

They checked the stairwell and the shared entrance. Sara called Milo softly, then stopped to listen. A washing machine hummed behind a closed door. Nothing moved on the stairs.

In the courtyard, their neighbour Dina was taking dry sheets from a line.

"Have you seen my cat this morning?" Sara asked.

"Remind me what he looks like."

"Grey, with white paws. His front left paw has a little grey patch on it."

"Big or small?"

"Quite small. No collar."

Sara opened a recent photograph on her phone. Milo lay across a newspaper, making most of the headline unreadable.

Dina studied it.

"I saw a grey cat by the bins, but that was yesterday. I can't say it was him."

"Thank you. Could you let me know if you see him again?"

"Of course. Have you checked the storage rooms?"

Sara looked toward the row of doors at the back of the courtyard. One had been open earlier, when someone was moving garden chairs.

The locked door

They did not know who used the first room. Dina knew that Mr Cole kept his gardening tools in the second, so she knocked at his flat and asked whether he could check it with them.

He came down carrying a ring of keys.

"I had the door open this morning", he said. "Only for a few minutes."

"That's all he'd need", Sara replied.

Mr Cole opened the room. They waited outside the doorway while Sara called. There were folded chairs, empty plant pots and a bag of compost. No cat appeared.

With Mr Cole's permission, they looked behind the larger things without moving the heavy stack of chairs. Sara crouched beside a pot and found a glove.

"One of mine", Mr Cole said. "Missing since March."

Sara tried to smile.

They left the room as they had found it. Mr Cole said he would keep an eye out and ask his wife about the other storage door.

Ben suggested they return to the flat for a moment. Sara was calling faster now, hardly leaving a gap between Milo's name and the next call.

"We can make a short notice with that photo", he said. "And check the flat once more."

The notice on the screen

At the kitchen table, Sara chose a photograph that showed all four paws. She wrote a description, the area where Milo had last been seen and a contact number.

Ben read it before she posted it in the building's residents group.

"You wrote that he escaped into the courtyard. We don't know that."

She changed it to say that he might have left through the front door that morning.

"Better?"

"Yes."

She asked neighbours to check enclosed spaces they had opened that day and to contact her if they saw him. Then she called her usual veterinary practice to tell them he was missing and check what information they needed. She noted their advice and checked that her contact details on Milo's microchip registration were current.

Her phone buzzed before she had put down the pen.

A message from Mr Cole.

"My wife says the first room belongs to the upstairs flat. They're home now."

A very small sound

The neighbour upstairs was called June. She had been putting away camping equipment that morning and came down with her key as soon as Sara explained.

At the first storage door, Sara heard a faint scrape from inside.

Everyone stopped talking.

Then came a short, annoyed sound that she knew very well.

June opened the door carefully. Milo was sitting on a folded blanket behind a camping bag, looking as if the delay had been someone else's fault.

Sara crouched and spoke to him. Ben brought the carrier from her flat while June kept the doorway clear. Once Milo had come close enough, Sara settled him into it and closed the door securely.

She stayed crouching for another moment, one hand on top of the carrier.

"There you are."

Dina, watching from the courtyard, took a sheet off her arm.

"Grey cat. White paws. Very unimpressed."

The update

Back upstairs, Milo drank some water and returned to the kitchen chair. Sara watched him settle, then sent an update to the residents group saying he had been found. She thanked everyone who had helped and let the veterinary practice know too.

Ben picked up the tennis ball from the table.

"We found this as well. A productive morning."

"Don't call it that yet."

Sara put a note beside the front door to remind herself to keep Milo in a closed room when carrying things in. Then she checked the latch and finally sat down.

Milo opened one eye from the chair opposite.

Ben lowered his voice.

"Should we tell him everyone was worried?"

"I think he's waiting for an apology."
