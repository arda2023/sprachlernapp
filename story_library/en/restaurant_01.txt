A table by the window

The reservation

Leah arrived twelve minutes early and checked her phone outside the restaurant. Her brother had sent a photograph of a bus shelter with the message "Nearly there."

There were six bus stops between that shelter and the restaurant. She put the phone away.

Inside, a waiter looked up from a book beside the entrance.

"Good evening. Do you have a reservation?"

"For two, at seven. Under Leah Morris. I asked for a table by the window."

He found her name.

"Yes, here we are. Your table is ready."

The table overlooked the square where Leah and her brother, Owen, used to wait for their father after work. The old cinema was now a furniture shop. Someone had painted the bus shelter yellow.

Leah had chosen the restaurant because Owen was moving away on Saturday. She wanted a proper evening together, with enough time to talk. At home, they usually spoke while one of them was carrying a box or looking for the scissors.

She hung her coat over the chair facing the window and sat down opposite it.

"Would you like a drink while you wait?" the waiter asked.

"Still water, please. A bottle for the table."

"Of course. My name's Tomas. Let me know if you need anything."

Two seats

Owen arrived at seven past seven with his coat over one arm and a paper bag under the other.

"Sorry. The bus stopped for ages."

"I saw where you were."

"Then you know I was on my way."

He put the bag beside his chair. Leah watched him look at the coat she had placed there.

"Is this my seat?"

"You get the view."

He sat, glanced at the window and opened his menu. Across the square, a delivery van was reversing toward the furniture shop. Its warning sound reached them through the glass.

"You can still see where the cinema sign used to be," Leah said.

Owen turned, then looked back at her.

"What?"

She repeated it. A group at the next table had begun laughing, and he leaned forward to hear her.

"The letters left marks on the wall," she said.

"Oh, yes."

He moved his chair closer to the table. Leah waited until the laughter subsided.

"Is something wrong?"

"No. It's a nice place."

"That wasn't what I asked."

He rubbed a thumb along the edge of the menu.

"It's a bit loud here. I want to hear what you're saying."

Leah looked at the window. She had booked the table almost three weeks earlier.

Then she looked at the empty corner beside a shelf of plants.

A different table

When Tomas returned, Leah asked whether the corner table was available.

"Certainly. Would you prefer that one?"

"If it's quieter."

"It usually is. You're farther from the large tables."

He moved the water and glasses while they collected their coats. Owen picked up the paper bag himself.

At the new table, he sat with his back to the wall.

"Better?" Leah asked.

"Much."

"I thought you'd like the window."

"I do. But I've seen the square before."

She laughed and finally opened her menu.

Tomas gave them a few minutes. When he came back, Owen asked about the soup.

"Is the tomato soup a starter or a main course?"

"The bowl on this menu is a starter. It comes with bread."

"I'll have that, then the chicken, please."

Leah chose the vegetable pie.

"Does it come with anything?"

"Potatoes and green beans."

"Could I have the dressing on the side?"

"Of course."

They ordered one portion of olives to share while they waited.

The paper bag

"What's in the bag?" Leah asked.

"Something from the kitchen cupboard."

"If it's another cable, I don't want it."

"Why would there be a cable in the kitchen cupboard?"

She looked at him.

"Fair point."

He took out a small blue bowl wrapped in a tea towel. There was a pale scratch inside it.

Leah recognized it at once. Their father used to put spare coins in it. On Saturdays, he would empty them into his hand and let the children decide whether they had enough for the cinema.

"I thought that got lost when we moved," she said.

"It was behind the serving dishes. I found it yesterday. Do you want it?"

"Don't you?"

"I have the big red one. And I can't take every bowl we own."

She turned it over. A small price sticker was still stuck to the bottom.

"He never took those off."

"He said nobody was supposed to look underneath."

Tomas arrived with the olives, and Owen moved the bowl safely onto the empty chair beside Leah's coat.

During dinner, they discussed Owen's new flat. Its kitchen had two cupboards, one of which contained the water heater. Leah offered him her narrow shelf. He asked for the measurements before saying yes.

She had expected to talk about how strange it would be when he left. Instead, she drew the shelf on the back of an old receipt while he explained where it might fit.

At the end

They shared a slice of lemon cake because neither wanted a whole dessert and both wanted to try it. Owen carefully divided the slice, then looked at the two pieces.

"That one's bigger."

"You cut it. I choose."

"I remember that rule."

When Tomas brought the bill, Leah reached for it.

"This one's mine. I invited you."

"Then breakfast on Saturday is mine. Before I go."

"Somewhere without boxes."

"Agreed."

She checked the bill and paid. Owen put the bowl back in its bag, but this time he handed it to her.

Outside, they stopped in front of the window. Another pair now sat at their first table, watching the square.

"It is a good view," Owen said.

Leah adjusted the bag so the bowl wouldn't knock against her keys.

"We can look while we wait for your bus."
