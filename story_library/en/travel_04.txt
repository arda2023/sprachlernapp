The missed train

Two coffees

When Sam returned with the coffees, the train was moving.

He stopped at the top of the stairs. The last carriage slid past a woman holding a bicycle, then disappeared beyond the end of the platform. Iris stood beside their suitcases with both hands in her coat pockets.

"Please tell me that wasn't ours."

"It was ours."

Sam looked at the cups. He had bought the larger size because the smaller one cost almost as much. The lids were warm against his fingers.

"You said quarter past."

"I said quarter to."

"No, you said we had time."

"We did, when you asked."

He offered her a coffee. She took it, but neither of them drank. The departure board changed, removing their train and replacing it with a service to somewhere they did not want to go.

They were travelling to a friend's wedding. The ceremony was not until the next morning, but they had promised to help arrange the tables that evening. Sam was carrying the place cards in a flat box inside his suitcase. Iris had the key to the house where several guests would sleep.

"I'll find the next one," she said.

Sam pulled out his phone.

"I can do that."

"Then do it."

The information desk

His search offered a fast train in forty minutes and a slower route with a change. He showed Iris the screen.

"The first one gets us there before six."

"Does our ticket work on it?"

Sam opened the ticket, then another page, then returned to the first. The small print did not become clearer when he enlarged it.

"Let's ask."

The information desk had a short queue. Ahead of them, a man was trying to describe a bag he had left on a train. Behind them, a child repeatedly pressed the handle of a wheeled suitcase until it clicked.

When their turn came, Sam explained that they had missed their departure. He did not mention the coffee.

The employee checked their tickets.

"These are for the train that has just left. I can look at the available options for you."

The fast train would require new tickets at a price Sam could not comfortably afford. The slower journey cost less but would get them to the wedding town after seven. They would need to change at a small station along the way.

"How long do we have for the connection?" Iris asked.

"Twenty eight minutes, according to this schedule. Here, I'll print it. Check the boards when you arrive, in case anything changes."

They moved aside with the paper. Sam counted the cost in his head. He had already paid for the journey, a room and a suit he would probably wear twice.

"I can't pay for the fast one," he said.

Iris looked at him, then at the printed route.

"The slower one is fine. We need to tell them when we'll arrive."

A message they could send

Sam started typing to their friend Daniel.

"Small delay with the train."

Iris leaned against a pillar.

"We're going to be several hours late."

He deleted the message.

"We missed our train. We'll arrive after seven. The place cards are with me. Is there anything we can do before we get there?"

Daniel replied with a photograph of a room full of unfolded tables.

"Tell me which guests are vegetarian. The list is on my laptop at home, and I only have the seating plan here."

Iris had a copy of the meal list in her email. She sent it, then called the guest who would reach the house first. They arranged for Daniel's brother to collect the spare key from the neighbour.

Sam watched her finish the call.

"I'm sorry about the train."

"I'm annoyed about the train. But I'm more annoyed that you expected me to sort everything out."

He opened his mouth, then closed it. She had booked the room, checked the route and reminded him to bring the box of cards. He had chosen the coffee.

"I'll handle the connection," he said.

"You can handle this train first."

They finally drank their coffees. Both were almost cold.

The station in between

On the slower train, Sam put the printed route on the little table. He checked the next station against the display, then set an alarm for ten minutes before their change.

Iris took off her coat and leaned back.

"Wake me if I fall asleep."

By the time they reached the connecting station, she was asleep. Sam touched her sleeve, waited until she had her bag and took both suitcases onto the platform.

The next train was listed on platform four. A notice beside the stairs said the lift was out of service. Sam found a staff member and asked whether there was another way across.

There was a ramp at the far end of the building. It took longer, but they had time. They walked together, the suitcase wheels rattling over the paving.

On platform four, Iris pointed to a kiosk.

"Coffee?"

Sam looked at the departure time.

"We have twelve minutes. I'm staying here."

"Good decision."

She bought two bottles of water instead.

A place at the table

Daniel met them outside the station after seven. There were folded napkins on the passenger seat of his car and a roll of tape around the gear lever.

"Don't ask," he said when Iris looked at it. "How was the journey?"

"Educational," she replied.

At the hall, someone had already laid the tablecloths. Sam opened his suitcase and lifted out the box. One corner was bent, but the cards inside were flat.

He and Iris walked between the tables, reading names and checking the seating plan. They disagreed once about which side counted as the front of table six. This time, Sam asked Daniel before moving anything.

Later, Daniel brought them plates of leftover pasta. Sam sat down beside Iris, with the empty card box under his chair.

"I've checked the return train," he said.

She waited.

"And I've sent you the time. In numbers."

Iris looked at the message on her phone. Then she passed him the bread.
