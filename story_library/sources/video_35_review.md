## Essential corrections

The transcript incorrectly presents the corporate death estimate as a WHO study. [The Examination's original analysis, 2 May 2026](https://www.theexamination.org/articles/deadliest-company-in-the-world), opened in full, attributes an estimated 57.3 million deaths during 1990 to 2023 to China Tobacco by multiplying the low end of Global Burden of Disease tobacco mortality estimates by a 97 percent market share. GBD is led by IHME at the University of Washington, not WHO. This is a modeled journalistic attribution, not a direct count or court finding. The underlying total includes secondhand smoke. The article acknowledges disputed assumptions and mortality uncertainty. Use only a short attributed summary, preserving limitations. Do not repeat its superlative as an independently established universal ranking. Requested comments were reportedly unanswered, which is not an admission.

[China CDC Weekly, Smoking Prevalence in Urban and Rural Areas, China 2024](https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/en/article/doi/10.46234/ccdcw2025.124), opened, with [full paper mirror](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228068/). The national survey found current smoking among people aged at least 15 declined from 26.6 percent in 2018 to 23.2 percent in 2024. The latter comprises 43.9 percent of males and 1.8 percent of females. Do not repeat that smoking has declined everywhere except China, or that every fifth Chinese person of all ages is a smoker. Distinguish smoking from all tobacco use, survey estimates from modeled standardized estimates, and prevalence from total cigarette sales. Progress and a large remaining burden can coexist.

[WHO China country profile 2025](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/country-profiles/tobacco/gtrc-2025/tobacco-2025-chn.pdf?download=true&sfvrsn=fbf1710d_3) uses a 2022 adult survey for its displayed prevalence table. Publication year is not survey year. The WHO China general topic page still displays 26.6 percent and should not override the newer dated survey.

## Public finances and institutions

The transcript compares combined taxes and profits with another company's pretax profit. These are different accounting quantities. Omit the Apple comparison, Saudi oil comparison, military funding equivalence and implication that gross receipts equal a net social benefit. Healthcare costs, lost work and household harm matter separately.

A [syndicated State Tobacco Monopoly Administration announcement](https://cn.2firsts.com/news/detail?id=13608), dated 6 March 2025, reports 2024 industry taxes and profits of 1.6008 trillion yuan and fiscal contributions of 1.5446 trillion yuan. Original regulator announcement not yet opened. Do not silently conflate these two totals, convert using an unspecified exchange rate or call both net company profit. Precise fiscal figures can be omitted pending original verification.

The State Tobacco Monopoly Administration and China National Tobacco Corporation are linked institutional identities, rather than an independent health regulator disciplining an unrelated firm. Explain the conflict plainly, while distinguishing monopoly administration from all public health functions. A [WHO hosted regional FCTC compliance review](https://extranet.who.int/fctcapps/sites/default/files/2023-11/WHO%20Framework%20Convention%20on%20Tobacco%20Control%20%28Articles%205_3%206%208%2011%20%26%2013%29%20Compliance%20of%20Asia-Pacific%20Countries.pdf) describes shared leadership. It is older evidence, not a current personnel roster. Avoid saying every government actor has the same aim or that the state becomes richer in a net economic sense with each cigarette.

## Health and treaty evidence

[NCI, light cigarettes and cancer risk](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/tobacco/light-cigarettes-fact-sheet), full primary text returned by search. Machine measured tar yields do not reliably measure human exposure. Light or low tar cigarettes are not safer. Explain in ordinary language without suggesting that a package number is a clinical measure of individual risk. Do not confuse light branding with occasional smoking.

[WHO FCTC China party record](https://extranet.who.int/fctcapps/fctcapps/fctc/implementation-database/parties/china) records ratification on 11 October 2005 and entry into force on 9 January 2006. China is the treaty party, not CNTC. Do not state that all 168 signatories ratified simultaneously. The transcript conflates signatures, parties and ratification.

[WHO statement on tobacco industry partnerships, 3 May 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/03-05-2023-who-and-tobacco-control-partners-urge-countries-not-to-partner-or-work-with-the-tobacco-industry) explains the conflict between industry commercial interests and public health policy. This supports the general institutional issue, not every allegation against CNTC.

Read treaty Article 11 and current packaging evidence before stating legal requirements. Distinguish binding text, implementation guidance and best practice. Do not imply the original treaty itself mandates the same graphic packaging design everywhere. Avoid an unsupported claim that plain packaging alone made exactly 100,000 Australians quit within one year.

## Claims requiring follow-up or omission

James B. Duke, mechanisation and entry into China can supply a short historical opening, but the atlas dialogue, exact machine speed, date and population need historical corroboration. Do not invent a witnessed conversation. Correct Durham and James B. Duke. British American Tobacco formation and the machinery episode are not one event. Mao anecdotes, brand preferences, Qing death penalties and military promises can be omitted rather than reproduced as fact without sources.

The documentary's branded packaging examples contain garbled names. Do not reconstruct uncertain names from transcription or reproduce promotional flavour descriptions. A general account of attractive packs, gifting and health warnings can retain the point. Do not claim that declining a cigarette is universally considered rude throughout China.

Claims of obstructed municipal rules, altered treaty translation, youth targeting, school sponsorship and international smuggling need their original investigation and responses. The Examination article links its September 2023 investigation, and the transcript cites OCCRP for illicit trade. Read those before retaining specifics. Describe allegations as reporting, not proven criminal convictions. Do not suggest all exported products are smuggled.

Omit unverified assertions that lung cancer patients universally pay over half their bills, that lawyers can no longer bring any tobacco case, or that all civic organizations have disappeared. Any discussion of political restrictions needs specific evidence rather than an absolute national generalization.

US settlement history requires distinguishing the 1998 Master Settlement Agreement, participating states, projected payments and actual payments over time. Do not say a one time payment of over 200 billion dollars already occurred without a dated source. The 1964 Surgeon General report is relevant if verified directly. Neither historical digression is essential to the central account.

Delete travel advertising, discount codes, eSIM and cybersecurity product claims, music cues and interviewer fragments. Preserve criticism of commercial incentives without stigmatizing people with nicotine dependence. Do not use consumer preference or state ownership as the sole causal explanation for a national epidemic.

## Intended structure

A large tobacco business largely unfamiliar outside its home market. Brief verified history of mass production and state consolidation. Explain the link between commercial and regulatory power, and why taxes are not the same as net benefit. Attribute the mortality model with its limitations. Discuss packaging and misleading low tar claims. Contrast treaty commitments with evidence of interference where verified. End with the real decline in prevalence, remaining harms and the possibility of stronger prevention, without predicting inevitable failure.
