## Narrative and attribution

The documentary explicitly relies substantially on Mitnick's autobiography. Preserve that limitation in the adaptation. Childhood bus adventures, precise telephone conversations, friendship disputes, private motives, the payroll prank, discovery of an informant and the arrest scene's clothing detail are not independently established by this review. Present selected autobiographical episodes as his account, not reconstructed dialogue or omniscient fact.

Describe a prominent early figure in public discussion of computer crime, not one of the first hackers in history. Do not equate a promotional most wanted description with membership of the FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list. Some contemporary articles repeat that claim without the underlying list record.

Keep both unauthorized intrusion and exaggerated public mythology visible. Curiosity and the absence of a demonstrated attempt to enrich himself do not remove privacy violations, victims' costs or the lack of consent. Conversely, do not treat every allegation circulating in newspapers as a conviction.

## Checked chronology and sources

[DOJ arrest announcement, 15 February 1995](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/Pre_96/February95/89.txt.html). Confirms arrest in Raleigh after an electronic investigation, a previous federal computer crime conviction and a warrant relating to release conditions. It is an arrest announcement: its additional allegations are not findings of guilt. Do not imply the 1995 arrest itself resolved every intrusion allegation.

[Federal Judicial Center, Special Needs Offenders Bulletin 5, August 2000, pages 3 and 4](https://www.fjc.gov/sites/default/files/2012/SNOBull5.pdf). Describes the earlier Digital case, subsequent investigation, separate cellular telephone fraud sentence, continued custody, 1999 agreement, restitution and supervised release. This is an official educational retrospective, not the complete docket. Some technical explanations are simplified; do not copy its description of Telnet as a telephone system.

[US Attorneys' Bulletin, May 2001, printed page 56](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/usao/legacy/2006/06/30/usab4903.pdf) reports a total sentence of 68 months. Contemporary sentencing reporting separately describes 46 months in the 1999 case. These are different scopes, not interchangeable numbers. Avoid the transcript's unqualified five and a half years as the single sentence and its implication that all detention since 1995 was exclusively pretrial. Distinguish sentence, other proceedings and time actually served. For the story, a clear account of the 1999 guilty plea and January 2000 release is preferable to unverified arithmetic across proceedings.

[Contemporary sentencing report, 10 August 1999](https://www.wired.com/1999/08/mitnick-could-go-free-in-january/) reports 46 months, seven counts across two federal indictments and $4,125 restitution. Its account explicitly connects the small payment to ability to pay. [Contemporary restitution hearing report](https://www.wired.com/1999/07/mitnick-case-is-down-to-dollars/) distinguishes the $1.5 million request from disputed wider loss claims. Never imply that $4,125 was the court's finding of the total harm, or that a claim of hundreds of millions was the amount of restitution formally requested. Precise loss valuation remains contested and is unnecessary to the narrative.

[Official Senate hearing transcript, 2 March 2000](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-106shrg63639/pdf/CHRG-106shrg63639.pdf) confirms his appearance after release. His statements about motives and methods are testimony by the subject, not independent verification of every past action. The Senate hearing landing page returned 403 when opened; the government PDF was available. Do not imply the entire 124 page hearing has been reviewed.

[Australian Institute of Criminology, restriction of use orders](https://www.aic.gov.au/publications/tandi/tandi286) describes broad technology restrictions with the possibility of prior express written approval. The FJC account specifies computer accessible televisions, not a universal ban on every ordinary television. Use substantial restrictions on computer and communications use and work, with authorised exceptions. Do not describe unconditional freedom immediately on release.

[KnowBe4 and family announcement, 20 July 2023](https://www.knowbe4.com/press/knowbe4-and-the-mitnick-family-honor-the-life-and-legacy-of-kevin-mitnick). Confirms death on 16 July 2023, age 59, after pancreatic cancer, and later employment as Chief Hacking Officer from 2011. This is an interested employer and family tribute. Use for dates and role, not proof that he invented social engineering or was objectively the world's best hacker.

## Technical and disputed details

Correct automatic transcription errors: Digital Equipment Corporation, DEC, VAX, VMS, Tsutomu Shimomura, Raleigh, CERT and John Markoff. Explain source code simply. Do not call magnetic tape an ancient hard drive. Do not claim access to every DEC computer worldwide merely from access to some connected systems.

The original CERT advisory CA-1995-01 is dated 23 January 1995, concerning IP spoofing and hijacked terminal connections. Located through the [CMU archive](https://resources.sei.cmu.edu/asset_files/whitepaper/1995_019_001_496168.pdf) and an [ACM RISKS reproduction](https://catless.newcastle.ac.uk/Risks/16/76). Both full-text opens failed in this review; search extracts identify the advisory and date. Do not claim full review. The warning establishes that attacks had been reported, not that Mitnick invented the method or that it had previously been merely theoretical. Avoid that unsupported first-in-history claim. A high level explanation of false network identity is sufficient.

Keep the Christmas 1994 intrusion, investigation and eventual arrest distinguishable. The documentary itself raises disputed attribution of taunting voicemail messages. Do not assign every voice message to Mitnick or present the rivalry as a literal two-person duel that excludes investigators and service providers.

The nuclear missile telephone story is part of Mitnick's later account of his treatment. No underlying hearing record establishing the prosecutor's exact statement was inspected. Omit the dramatic claim or explicitly identify it as his disputed recollection, never an actual technical capability or verified judicial finding.

No detailed call scripts, commands for account takeover, credential collection, monitoring suppression, backdoor installation, exfiltration routing, evidence destruction or interception of private calls are needed for this language-learning biography. Retain the historical acts and consequences at a descriptive level. Remove the sponsorship passage, discount offer, lyrics and music cues.
