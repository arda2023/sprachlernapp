## Medical corrections

[WHO guideline recommendations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK285525/) and [WHO explanation, 4 March 2015](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children) distinguish free sugars from all sugars. Strong recommendation: less than 10 percent of energy from free sugars. Conditional recommendation: further reduction below 5 percent for additional benefit. For a 2,000 kcal illustrative diet these correspond to 50 g and 25 g. Do not call 25 g a universal maximum for every person or count all naturally occurring milk sugar against it. Free sugars include added sugar, honey, syrups and juice sugars, whereas sugars inside intact fruit and naturally present in milk are outside that definition. The transcript's breakfast sum mixes categories and unspecified portions. Remove the 55 g calculation or rebuild with explicit products and free sugar assumptions. Avoid claiming the average German consumes three times a universal WHO limit.

[NIDDK insulin resistance and prediabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/what-is-diabetes/prediabetes-Insulin-resistance) lists multiple risk factors. Explain type 2 diabetes without reducing it to eating sugar or blaming patients. Do not confuse type 1 diabetes with diet-related risk. Sugar-containing drinks can contribute to excess energy and are associated with type 2 risk, but a single food does not explain every case. Needs additional systematic review for precise beverage risk claims before drafting.

Remove the prehistoric sweet berry safety inference. Sweet taste is not a reliable test of edibility, and a fictional prehistoric person cannot establish an evolutionary history. If an illustrative opening is used, mark it as imagined and do not include unsafe foraging advice. Prefer an ordinary modern shopping or breakfast illustration.

Avoid unsupported all-purpose claims that sugar directly causes Alzheimer's, cancer or psychiatric illness. Distinguish evidence about dental caries, excess energy intake, adiposity and cardiometabolic risk from weaker or indirect associations. Do not say all whole fruit is unlimited or uniformly harmless for every individual. Explain food structure, fibre and dietary pattern without an absolute rule that all added sugar always causes a rapid glucose spike.

Reward and craving are not by themselves a clinical substance dependence diagnosis. Do not equate sugar with tobacco or alcohol, or claim no person can experience symptoms after a dietary change. Describe difficulty moderating intake without declaring an established sugar addiction diagnosis. Energy balance remains relevant, while satiety and food environment affect intake. Avoid presenting those as competing physical laws.

## Historical research

[Kearns and colleagues, 2016 historical analysis, original paper mirror](https://math.colorado.edu/~walter/MATH2380/Resources/sugar.heart.2016.pdf) describes Project 226. Correct name D. Mark Hegsted, with Robert McGandy and Frederick Stare. The 1967 publications were a two-part literature review, not two new clinical experiments. Sponsorship was not disclosed. The review discounted sugar evidence, rather than never mentioning sugar. Avoid unsupported fictional office meetings or dialogue. The original reported payment was $6,500, equivalent to $48,900 in 2016 dollars, not an undated modern $67,000. Prefer original amount or omit money. Journal disclosure rules at the time differed from modern requirements. Funding influence is not proof that every conclusion about saturated fat was false.

[USDA historical guidelines table](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/publications/42215/5831_aib750b_1_.pdf?v=57009) explicitly shows the 1980 recommendation to avoid too much sugar. [HHS 1980 document landing page](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/dietary-guidelines/previous-dietary-guidelines/1980) identifies the original. The transcript's implication that federal guidance simply ignored sugar while condemning fat is misleading. Do not claim one paid review determined decades of guidelines or that all low-fat products replaced fat with sugar.

[Project 259 historical analysis, PLOS Biology, 21 November 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.2003460) concerns animal research, intestinal microbes, blood lipids and an enzyme associated with bladder cancer. It is not a human trial proving that sugar causes bladder cancer. Authors found funding termination and no published results. Attribute the interpretation of suppression and motive to the historians' analysis rather than presenting a reconstructed meeting as witnessed fact. Preserve uncertainty about inference from internal documents. A brief paragraph is enough.

[1971 National Caries Program historical analysis, PLOS Medicine](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article%3Fid%3D10.1371/journal.pmed.1001798) analysed industry and agency documents. Its 78 percent figure concerns a submitted industry report incorporated into the first request for research proposals, not 78 percent of all government policy or all dental research. It found emphasis on approaches permitting continued sugar consumption and omission of food-specific cariogenicity research. Funding and priority selection can shape knowledge without fabricated experimental results. Avoid labelling all participants corrupt as a proven legal fact.

## Additional evidence review before drafting

[BMJ systematic review, 2015, h3576](https://www.bmj.com/content/351/bmj.h3576) examined 17 prospective cohorts with 38,253 type 2 diabetes cases. Higher sweetened beverage consumption was associated with greater incidence even after adjustment for adiposity. This is observational evidence, not a randomized proof that one serving causes diabetes. Population attributable estimates explicitly assume causality. Draft should describe the association without a personal risk percentage or implying that artificially sweetened beverages necessarily cause diabetes, since confounding and reverse causality matter.

[WHO child food marketing guideline, 3 July 2023](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240075412) supports effects on children's food choices, intake, purchase requests and norms. [WHO and UNICEF implementation toolkit](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240047518/) explains the policy context. These support discussing marketing beyond individual willpower. They do not establish a universal daily count of advertisements or imply parents have no agency.

[European Commission food information legislation explanation](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/food-information-consumers-legislation_en?prefLang=de) confirms the regulation applied from 13 December 2014, with the nutrition information obligation from 13 December 2016 for the majority of prepacked processed foods. Do not call this the beginning of all mandatory food labelling. Total sugar on a nutrition declaration is not a separate measurement of free sugars. Use no claim that the label alone provides the latter.

[2013 PLOS review of systematic reviews](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1001578) analysed 17 reviews containing 18 conclusions. Of 12 conclusions without a reported financial conflict, ten found an adverse association. Of six with a potential industry conflict, five judged the evidence insufficient to establish an association. Thus the two 83.3 percent figures do not concern hundreds of experiments or a finding of protective effects. For readability, prefer a small-sample qualitative summary with attribution, not precise percentages detached from denominators. Funding does not automatically invalidate results, and reported absence of conflict is not proof of exhaustive independence.

[Original 2023 interim evaluation of Germany's reduction strategy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10568594/) and [publisher version](https://karger.com/anm/article/79/3/282/835857/Interim-Evaluation-of-Germany-s-Sugar-Reduction) report sales-weighted soft drink sugar concentration decreasing about 2 percent in Germany from 2015 to 2021, compared with 29 percent in the UK in the same dataset. This is an interim assessment, not the final measured outcome for 2025. Do not conflate concentration with beverage volume or per capita sugar sales. The 15 percent target was for 2025, with 2015 as the baseline. The separate UK official 2015 to 2024 report has a different period and scope, so its 47.4 percent result must not be directly paired with Germany's 2021 result as if periods and definitions match. Prefer the matched 2015 to 2021 comparison in the story.

[Official second interim report landing page, 25 April 2024](https://www.publikationen-bundesregierung.de/pp-de/publikationssuche/reduktion-salz-fett-zucker-2269112) confirms existence, date and scope, including stakeholder assessments. Full PDF not read in this turn. Do not attribute specific report conclusions beyond what has been inspected. Original 2023 study suffices for the numerical comparison.

[Chile before-and-after study, PLOS Medicine, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371%2FJOURNAL.PMED.1003015) found a 23.7 percent reduction in household purchases of beverages classified as high-in relative to a counterfactual based on prior trends, for 2015 to 2017. This is not a 23.7 percent fall in all beverage purchases or all sugar intake. Other beverage purchases rose. The package combined warning labels, marketing restrictions and school sales rules, so it cannot isolate one component. Prefer a qualitative explanation of the combined policy and observed purchasing change. No claim that the observational design proves every downstream health effect.
