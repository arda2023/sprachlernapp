## Writing constraints

Use reported prisoner accounts with clear attribution. Do not reproduce self injury technique, medication advice, graphic humiliation, reconstructed dialogue or specific violent mechanics. A prisoner describes distress and alleged deprivation, not a model for obtaining medical care. Treatment disputes should not become a medical recommendation.

Explain special secure cells as exceptional preventive measures whose justification and duration require scrutiny. Do not say the absence of a simple fixed limit authorises indefinite punishment. Verify any precise statutory rule or reform before asserting it is in force. Proposed judicial approval and planned protective rooms must not silently become enacted law.

Avoid categorical guilt, a definitive national scandal ranking, psychiatric labels, speculation about undiscovered victim totals or the assumption all staff knew. The reported conviction of one prisoner in an earlier confrontation does not itself settle the later allegations against officers. If retained, attribute both competing accounts and the reported outcome without pretending to have inspected that judgment.

Do not infer that receipt of a letter by a ministry proves personal knowledge by the minister. The institutional handling of complaints and individual criminal responsibility are related but distinct questions. Keep the narrative focused on the concrete warnings, investigation and people affected rather than broad claims about every German prison.
